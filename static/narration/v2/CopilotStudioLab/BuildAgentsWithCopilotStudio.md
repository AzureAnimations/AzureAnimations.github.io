## Step 1 · Describing an agent is easy

[confident] Welcome to the PL-7008 Copilot Studio labs.
[600ms]
[calm] Describing an agent is easy — one sentence, and you have something working.

## Step 2 · Five labs, four hours

[confident] Five labs, about four hours of hands-on time.
[600ms]
[calm] Creating agents and managing topics are forty-five minutes each. Knowledge and workflow tools are an hour. The new experience is a quick thirty.
[700ms]

## Step 3 · One agent, five moving parts

[calm] Every Copilot Studio agent is the same five things.
[600ms]
[confident] Instructions say how it behaves. Knowledge gives it something true to answer from. Topics pin down conversations that must go a certain way. Tools let it act. Channels decide where people find it.
[700ms]
[curious] Each lab switches one of these on.

## Step 4 · Where the pieces live

[calm] The agent is one object in Copilot Studio, but the labs reach well beyond it.
[600ms]
[confident] Grounding data sits in Dataverse, in files, and on public websites. Actions run as flows in Power Automate. The finished agent surfaces in Teams.
[700ms]
[encouraging] Toggle any lab to place the pieces it builds.

## Step 5 · Explore the labs

[confident] This is the map of the course, and it is yours to wander.
[600ms]
[calm] Every panel shows the same three things. What you build, what you can do once it is finished, and the mistake that catches most people out.
[700ms]
[encouraging] Start wherever you like. With only an hour, lab one then lab five is the sharpest contrast.

## Step 6 · What you can do at the end

[proud] By the end you have built agents both ways.
[600ms]
[confident] The classic way — grounded in files and Dataverse, held to a script by topics, acting through workflows you authored.
[600ms]

[reassuring] The real skill is knowing which one a problem needs.
