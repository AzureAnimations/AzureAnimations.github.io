# Narrator Script — Journey · Microsoft 365 Copilot in Word

**Source animation:** `static/v2/Copilot/CopilotInWord.html`

Tags in `[brackets]` are delivery cues — speaking styles (e.g. `[confident]`),
paralinguistics (e.g. `[laughter]`), and `[NNNms]` pause markers (e.g. `[600ms]`).
One block per animation step.

---

## Step 1 · A blank page and a deadline

[concerned] Priya Nair, the project manager, asks Alex for an executive proposal for Contoso's new AI Customer Support Platform.
[600ms]
[calm] The material already exists — notes, strategy documents, feedback reports, all of it scattered.
[600ms]
[curious] What doesn't exist is the proposal, and the executives expect it this week.
[800ms]
[reflective] The hard part is rarely the writing. It's the starting.

## Step 2 · Copilot lives inside Word

[confident] Alex selects Copilot on the ribbon, and a pane opens right beside the document.
[600ms]
[calm] The prompt is ordinary English: create a proposal for an AI Customer Support Platform.
[600ms]
[impressed] Draft with Copilot writes straight onto the page — an executive summary, the opportunity, the proposed solution, and next steps.
[800ms]
[reassuring] No separate tool, no special commands. It's the document Alex is already writing.

## Step 3 · Chat Only, or Allow Editing

[serious] Before anything gets written, there's a choice worth understanding.
[600ms]
[calm] In Chat Only, Alex asks what sections the proposal should include, and Copilot answers — the document stays untouched.
[600ms]
[confident] Switch to Allow Editing, ask for an executive summary, and now Copilot writes into the page itself.
[800ms]
[reassuring] Ask for advice first; hand over the pen when you're ready. Alex can switch back at any time.

## Step 4 · Ground the draft in your own files

[curious] A first draft is tidy — but it could belong to any company.
[800ms]
[determined] So Alex types a forward slash in the pane and references real files: A I Strategy F Y twenty-seven, the Project Phoenix Proposal, the Customer Feedback Report.
[600ms]
[impressed] Copilot can draw on referenced documents, emails and meetings totalling up to seventy-five thousand words.
[600ms]
[confident] Now the draft speaks Contoso's language — specific beats generic, every time.

## Step 5 · Rewrite it for the audience

[concerned] There's a problem. The draft reads like an engineering document — a REST inference endpoint, sub two-hundred millisecond latency, autoscaled across three regions.
[600ms]
[determined] Alex selects that paragraph and asks Copilot to rewrite it for executive leadership.
[600ms]
[impressed] Back comes the same fact in business language: customers get an answer in under a second, and the service stays fast as demand grows.
[800ms]
[calm] Same facts, different reader — and the original stays until Alex accepts the change.

## Step 6 · Turn one line into a section

[reflective] One sentence sits in the draft: an AI chatbot will improve customer support.
[600ms]
[curious] True — but that isn't a business case.
[800ms]
[determined] Alex asks Copilot to expand it with business benefits and expected outcomes.
[600ms]
[impressed] The line grows into a section — faster first response, round-the-clock coverage, and agents freed for the cases only people can handle — and Alex decides what stays.

## Step 7 · Read a 50-page document in seconds

[concerned] Then a customer sends through a requirements document. Fifty-two pages of it.
[600ms]
[confident] Alex asks Copilot to summarise it, and Word reads the whole thing.
[600ms]
[impressed] The goals, the requirements, the risks, the timeline and the action items — cut first-response time by half, Entra sign-in and E U data residency, a pilot in Q two, and a budget owner to confirm by Friday.
[800ms]
[calm] A summary before Alex reads a single line of it.

## Step 8 · Ask the document questions

[curious] The proposal now runs to several pages — so why scroll through it?
[800ms]
[confident] Alex simply asks: what are the top risks?
[600ms]
[impressed] Copilot answers from this document — model accuracy at launch, integration with the existing ticketing system, and change management for the support team — and shows the sections it drew each point from.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Talk to the document instead of searching through it.

## Step 9 · A second pair of eyes

[serious] This proposal is about to land in front of Contoso's executives.
[600ms]
[determined] So Alex asks Copilot to review it and suggest improvements.
[600ms]
[impressed] Back comes a checklist — structure, clarity, tone, grammar, missing information, executive readiness — with one specific fix: add the expected R O I to the executive summary, because leadership will look for it first.
[800ms]
[reassuring] Copilot is a writing partner. The final call is always Alex's.

## Step 10 · From a blank page to an executive proposal

[proud] Request, blank page, guidance, sources, draft, rewrite, expand, summarise, review.
[600ms]
[confident] One document, one pane, one conversation — grounded in Contoso's own files rather than invented.
[600ms]
[impressed] Without Copilot that's think, research, draft, rewrite, review. With it: prompt, refine, approve.
[800ms]
[encouraging] Proposal dot docx is ready — and in the next stop, that approved document becomes the deck.
