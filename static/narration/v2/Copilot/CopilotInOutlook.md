Tags in `[brackets]` are delivery cues — speaking styles (e.g. `[confident]`),

## Step 1 · Three hundred unread, a week of the project inside

[concerned] Alex is back at Contoso after a week away, and three hundred unread messages are waiting.
[600ms]
[curious] Somewhere in there is the AI Customer Support Platform — but where do you even start?
[800ms]
[confident] Instead of opening them one by one, Alex asks Copilot in the Outlook sidebar to summarise the important emails.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Four need an answer, twenty-three are just FYI — and Copilot only ever reads the mail Alex could already open.

## Step 2 · Fifty messages, one decision

[concerned] The pilot-scope thread has run fifty messages, eleven people, two weeks — and nobody wrote a recap.
[600ms]
[confident] Alex asks Copilot to summarise the conversation, and back comes what the thread actually decided.
[600ms]
[impressed] EMEA first, four weeks, scoped to setup questions — and Finance approved the pilot budget, not the full rollout.
[800ms]
[calm] One thing is still open: who owns the escalation path. Alex reads that in seconds, not in forty-six messages.

## Step 3 · Alex jots it, Copilot writes it

[amused] Between meetings, Alex bangs out a few broken words — no capitals, no sentences, barely a reply.
[600ms]
[confident] He selects them, chooses Edit with Copilot, and asks for a rewrite.
[600ms]
[impressed] The same points come back in full sentences: the pilot confirmed, the escalation path owned, a plan before Thursday.
[800ms]
[reassuring] Copilot never needed a blank page — it worked from the words Alex already wrote, and Alex still reviews before sending.

## Step 4 · Same message, different room

[curious] The words are right — but is this the version a steering committee should read?
[800ms]
[confident] One draft, rewritten on request: make it shorter, make it friendlier, make it more formal.
[600ms]
[impressed] Copilot keeps the same message and simply refits it, and coaching tips rate the clarity, the sentiment and the tone.
[600ms]
[calm] They even flag the one deadline that's still vague — so Alex names the day before pressing send.

## Step 5 · Walking in already briefed

[determined] The steering committee is tomorrow, and Alex asks Copilot to prepare for it.
[600ms]
[confident] Copilot gathers the related emails, the documents and the earlier discussions into a single meeting brief.
[600ms]
[impressed] They approved the pilot, not the full rollout; last time they asked what success looks like; and the escalation owner is still Alex's to name.
[800ms]
[serious] Meeting preparation in Outlook does need a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence — but with it, Outlook stops being a mailbox and starts being preparation.

## Step 6 · From 300 unread to four answers and a plan

[proud] One morning back at the desk, and the whole week has landed.
[600ms]
[confident] The inbox is triaged, the long thread has a conclusion, the reply is sent, and tomorrow's committee is already prepared for.
[600ms]
[optimistic] Summarise, respond, move forward — without reading all three hundred.
[800ms]
[encouraging] Copilot works only on the mail and files Alex can already open, and Alex stays the one who decides what to send.
