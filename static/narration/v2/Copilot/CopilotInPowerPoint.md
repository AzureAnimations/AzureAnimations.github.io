# Narrator Script — Journey · Microsoft 365 Copilot in PowerPoint

**Source animation:** `static/v2/Copilot/CopilotInPowerPoint.html`

Tags in `[brackets]` are delivery cues — speaking styles (e.g. `[confident]`),
paralinguistics (e.g. `[laughter]`), and `[NNNms]` pause markers (e.g. `[600ms]`).
One block per animation step.

---

## Step 1 · The proposal is approved — now they want the deck

[optimistic] Good news lands from Priya Nair, the project manager: the proposal for the AI Customer Support Platform is approved.
[600ms]
[concerned] The catch is the next sentence. The executive steering committee meets on Thursday, and they want slides.
[600ms]
[confident] So Alex points Copilot at the document itself — Proposal dot docx, twelve approved pages — and asks for a presentation.
[800ms]
[calm] The thinking is already done. It only has to become a deck.

## Step 2 · Point Copilot at the file — and at the template

[determined] In a brand-new, empty presentation, Alex asks Copilot to create from a file.
[600ms]
[confident] Typing a forward slash brings up the recent files. Alex picks the approved Proposal dot docx — and Copilot can reference up to five files if a story needs more than one.
[600ms]
[impressed] Then the template: Contoso Executive, straight from the organisation's collection. Generate.
[800ms]
[reassuring] Five slides, laid out, already on brand — because Alex named the file and the template up front.

## Step 3 · Put it on the company template

[calm] A draft deck still isn't a Contoso deck.
[600ms]
[confident] Alex opens the organisation's template gallery — Contoso Standard, Contoso Executive, Contoso Product — and asks for the executive one.
[600ms]
[impressed] Copilot rebuilds the same slides with the approved brand colours, the approved fonts, the Contoso logo and the executive layouts.
[800ms]
[reassuring] Copilot follows the presentation standards, so nobody restyles twenty slides by hand.

## Step 4 · Ask for a slide that isn’t there yet

[intrigued] The committee will ask what the business actually gets out of this — and there's no slide for that.
[800ms]
[determined] One prompt: create a slide explaining the business benefits.
[600ms]
[impressed] Copilot builds it. First response in minutes, not hours. Support around the clock. Agents freed for the complex cases — with icons, and speaker notes to go with them.
[600ms]
[confident] This isn't reshaping what Alex already wrote. It's new content, from one sentence of intent.

## Step 5 · Say less, land more

[concerned] The solution slide is a wall of text, lifted straight out of the proposal — four dense bullets, fifty words.
[600ms]
[determined] Alex asks Copilot to simplify it for executives.
[600ms]
[impressed] Back comes the same slide as three lines, twenty-seven words. An assistant that answers from our own knowledge base. Plugged into the ticketing system we already use. Region by region, with a person always one click away.
[800ms]
[calm] An executive reads a slide in about four seconds. Copilot writes for those four seconds.

## Step 6 · Make the point visible

[reflective] Three tidy lines are still three lines of text.
[600ms]
[confident] So Alex asks Copilot to suggest visuals, and Designer — built right into Copilot — offers four ways to show it.
[600ms]
[impressed] A chart of response time before and after. An icon layout, one per benefit. A process diagram from question to assistant to agent. Or a brand-approved image from the SharePoint asset library.
[800ms]
[reassuring] Alex picks; Copilot applies the layout. That's help with the storytelling, not just the writing.

## Step 7 · Ask the deck before the room does

[concerned] Twenty slides, and a steering committee that will pick at every one of them.
[800ms]
[curious] So Alex asks the deck first: which slides are too detailed?
[600ms]
[impressed] Slides three and six carry the most text — move that detail into the speaker notes and keep one message on each. And Copilot points straight at them: Architecture, and Rollout plan.
[600ms]
[confident] Summarise this presentation. What are the key messages? Rehearse with Copilot, because it reads the deck the way an executive will.

## Step 8 · From an approved document to a deck the committee will read

[proud] One approved proposal, one prompt, one template, and a handful of refinements.
[600ms]
[confident] Create, restyle, extend, simplify, illustrate and interrogate — all inside PowerPoint, in plain language.
[600ms]
[impressed] Without Copilot that's re-read, copy, restyle, rewrite, rehearse. With it: prompt, refine, present.
[800ms]
[encouraging] PowerPoint is where the story gets told — and Alex is still the presenter, and still the one who approves every slide.
