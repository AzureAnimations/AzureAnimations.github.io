# Explore Microsoft Copilot — narration (en)

## Step 1 · You have an idea, not a business

[confident] Every business starts the same way. Someone writes the idea down.
[500ms]
[calm] Here it is: one Word document, a few rough notes about starting a commercial cleaning company in New York City.
[400ms]
[curious] But an idea in a document is not a business. There is no plan to circulate, no numbers to show an investor, no presentation to pitch with, and no meeting in the diary.
[500ms]
[helpful] Over the next forty minutes you close all four of those gaps, without writing a single line of code.

## Step 2 · Five tasks, one business

[confident] The lab is five tasks, and then a challenge you take as far as you like.
[500ms]
[calm] The five core tasks are the ones that build the business: research the idea in Word, draft the plan, chart the numbers in Excel, generate the deck in PowerPoint, and book the funding meeting in Outlook.
[400ms]
[helpful] The sixth is optional. It hands you the same loop with no prompts written down, so you can run it on a subject of your own.
[500ms]
[confident] Notice the order along the bottom: idea, plan, numbers, deck, meeting. Each task picks up where the last one stopped.

## Step 3 · What you walk away with

[calm] This is the part the instructions never quite show you: the five things that actually exist when you are finished.
[400ms]
[confident] The idea document, summarised and restyled. A business plan drafted from it. A workbook of projections charted from the plan. A deck built from the story. And the email that asks for the money.
[500ms]
[curious] Look at the arrows. Nothing here is generated from nothing.
[400ms]
[helpful] That is the real lesson of this lab. Copilot is at its most useful when it can read what you already made.

## Step 4 · Where all of this happens

[calm] Three places, and that is the whole picture.
[400ms]
[confident] On the left, your browser: the Microsoft 365 portal, and a tab for Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook. In the middle, Copilot itself, living inside each of those apps. On the right, your OneDrive, holding the files they all share.
[500ms]
[helpful] Use the buttons underneath to light up what each task touches. Tap a task to add it, tap it again to take it away, and build the picture in whatever order you like.
[400ms]
[curious] One thing is worth knowing before you start. Copilot can only see a file if it is in OneDrive. That is exactly why the lab has you upload the idea document before anything else.

## Step 5 · Explore each task

[confident] Now the tasks themselves. Pick any of them, in any order.
[500ms]
[calm] Every panel shows you the same three things: which app you open and where the Copilot box is, the prompt you actually type, and what comes back when you send it.
[400ms]
[helpful] Underneath each one is the capability you have just earned, and the single mistake that catches most people out on that task.
[500ms]
[curious] The prompts are a starting point, not a script. Change them, ask follow-up questions, and keep going until the answer is the one you wanted. That is the whole point of the exercise.

## Step 6 · From one document to a funded pitch

[confident] And there it is. One page of notes at the start, and at the end a plan, a forecast, a deck and a meeting request.
[500ms]
[calm] The five core tasks take about forty minutes. The challenge is open ended, and it is the one worth doing on something that matters to you.
[400ms]
[helpful] Each task fed the next one. That chain, rather than any single clever prompt, is what makes Copilot worth having.
