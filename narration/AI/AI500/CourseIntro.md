## Step 1 · Hook

[curious] Meet Solo, the fictional agent handling every insurance claim at Contoso.
[600ms]
[calm] Reading documents, checking policies, investigating fraud, and approving payments all land on the same agent.
[600ms]
[serious] As the workload grows, the warning signs show why we need to rethink the system, not just write a longer prompt.

## Step 2 · Question

[confident] Now the work is shared by specialist agents, with an Orchestrator coordinating the team.
[600ms]
[curious] Intake, Policy, Fraud, and Payout have different jobs, but how will they share information and decide what happens next?
[600ms]
[reassuring] Keep the Human Reviewer in view, because coordinating a team also means deciding when a person must take over.

## Step 3 · AI-500

[confident] AI-500 focuses on designing and implementing multi-agent AI solutions.
[600ms]
[calm] The certification card names the Multi-Agent AI Solutions Expert credential, which recognizes expertise in production-ready systems.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Think beyond a successful demo: the system must also scale, recover from failures, and operate within its safety boundaries.

## Step 4 · Four verbs

[calm] The building gives us four verbs to remember: architect, develop, evaluate, and secure.
[600ms]
[confident] First design the team, then build its prompts, memory, and tools, and prove that the whole workflow works.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Security and deployment complete the picture, with Microsoft Foundry and Azure forming the foundation beneath all four floors.

## Step 5 · Ladder

[calm] This ladder places the expert credential above the basics of building and operating AI solutions.
[600ms]
[confident] For the credential, passing AI-500 is only one part: Microsoft also requires the prerequisite certification shown on its official page.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Use the ladder to plan your learning, and check Microsoft Learn rather than treating every displayed rung as a mandatory exam.

## Step 6 · Who

[confident] This course is aimed at practitioners who already understand agents and production systems.
[600ms]
[calm] The checklist includes Python, Microsoft Foundry, Azure services, and familiar tools and standards such as MCP and RAG.
[600ms]
[encouraging] If those foundations still feel new, strengthen them first so you can focus on design decisions rather than basic setup.

## Step 7 · Approach

[curious] The two lenses ask different questions: how do I write the code, and which design should I choose?
[600ms]
[calm] Sequential or parallel execution, shared or isolated memory, and release strategies all depend on the workload and its constraints.
[600ms]
[confident] Read the code as evidence, then explain why the chosen design fits better than the alternatives.

## Step 8 · Depth

[calm] The learning pyramid illustrates a shift from remembering features to evaluating and creating solutions.
[600ms]
[curious] In the example scenario, the fastest or cheapest answer is not automatically best, because both constraints still matter.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Practice explaining trade-offs; this is a learning illustration, not a promise about the exact questions in your exam.

## Step 9 · Recap

[confident] We have moved from one overloaded agent to a coordinated team with clear responsibilities.
[600ms]
[calm] The recap brings together the audience, the four domains, and the habit of choosing designs that satisfy real constraints.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Next, explore each floor of the building and use the official study guide to keep your preparation current.
