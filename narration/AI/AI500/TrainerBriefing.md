# Narrator Script — AI-500 · Trainer Briefing

**Source animation:** [TrainerBriefing.html](../../../journeys/AI/AI500/TrainerBriefing.html)
**Language:** en

Delivery cues in brackets are not spoken. Pause markers separate sentences.
Sources: [AI-500 study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/ai-500), [Expert certification](https://learn.microsoft.com/credentials/certifications/multi-agent-ai-solutions-expert/)., [Exam scoring](https://learn.microsoft.com/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports).
Trainer guidance and the illustrated agenda are teaching choices, not guarantees of exam format.
These scripts are prepared in five languages; the animation currently offers English only.

## Step 1 · Positioning

[confident] Position this course as designing systems of agents, not teaching someone their first agent.
[600ms]
[calm] The three contrasts distinguish architecture and trade-offs from syntax lessons and feature memorization.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Set that expectation early so learners understand why explaining a decision matters just as much as completing a lab.

## Step 2 · Days

[calm] The day-to-domain map explains why a classroom schedule is not a one-to-one exam checklist.
[600ms]
[confident] Development stretches across the first two days in this illustrated agenda, and its domain has the largest weight.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Return to the official domain weights when setting revision priorities, and confirm the actual course schedule before delivery.

## Step 3 · Scope

[serious] The two buckets separate the scope of this training from the objectives explicitly listed in the study guide.
[600ms]
[calm] Some deeper discussions help learners make better decisions even when the exam does not name them as separate objectives.
[600ms]
[confident] When asked whether something is examined, verify the current skills list rather than treating this teaching illustration as a permanent contract.

## Step 4 · Mix-ups

[curious] Flip a card to expose a distinction learners often overlook.
[600ms]
[calm] Compare tool protocols with agent protocols, different memory lifetimes, orchestration hierarchies, context failures, and release patterns.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Pause on a pair and ask learners to explain what changes in a real workflow before you reveal the clarification.

## Step 5 · Delivery

[confident] Keep the class anchored to Contoso's claims scenario so each module adds to the same story.
[600ms]
[curious] After a lab, ask what would change under a much higher load or stricter tenant requirements.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Use the player to pause at a failure or guardrail, and let learners predict the next step before revealing it.

## Step 6 · Check first

[serious] This checklist gathers the facts that should be refreshed before you teach.
[600ms]
[calm] Check exam status, duration, pricing, prerequisite wording, practice-assessment availability, and the status of preview features.
[600ms]
[confident] Use current Microsoft Learn pages for those details rather than quoting an old slide or relying on memory.

## Step 7 · FAQ

[calm] Open one question at a time and use the short answer as the start of a discussion.
[600ms]
[confident] The themes are preparation, coding proficiency, framework choices, credential requirements, and staying current after the course.
[600ms]
[encouraging] Connect each answer to the learner's existing experience, then point to official guidance whenever a requirement or exam detail is involved.
