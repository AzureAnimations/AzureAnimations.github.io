## Step 1 · Welcome

[confident] Welcome to AI Economics and FinOps.
[600ms]
Artificial intelligence is changing how organizations work, but adopting it is not only a technology decision. Every request to an AI service has a cost, and every cost needs a reason.
[700ms]
[calm] This journey is built around a simple loop, which you can see in the diagram. People and applications use AI. That usage creates costs. Governance, meaning budgets, alerts and clear ownership, keeps those costs visible. Optimization does the same work for less. And together they lead to business value, which then shapes the next round of AI usage.
[800ms]
[encouraging] You will learn each part of that loop, try it with interactive examples, and finish with a short knowledge check. I will walk you through the page as you go.

## Step 2 · Why it matters

[confident] Here is the core idea: AI innovation needs financial understanding.
[600ms]
As soon as an organization moves beyond a pilot, four questions appear. How much will an AI solution cost? Why do costs change as usage grows? How do we monitor and control spending? And how do we know whether AI delivers real value?
[700ms]
[calm] Notice that each card answers its question in plain language. Cost needs a range, not a guess, because usage, model choice and growth all move the number. Many AI services charge for what you use, so more people, longer requests and new automations all add up. Control comes from visibility, budgets, alerts and ownership, so surprises show up early instead of on the invoice. And value comes from choosing success measures up front, then comparing them with the cost.
[800ms]
[reassuring] The key message is that successful adoption takes more than powerful technology. It takes financial awareness, responsible management, and a clear view of business value.

## Step 9 · Stage 1 · Understand AI investment

[confident] Stage one asks a basic question: what are we investing in?
[600ms]
Organizations buy and consume AI in different ways. Some pay a fixed fee per user, which is a subscription. Others pay for what they use, which is usage-based. Knowing which model you are on changes how you plan, budget and monitor.
[700ms]
[calm] In the interactive, move the sliders for the number of employees and the requests per person each day. Watch which model costs less. The point is that the cheaper option changes as usage changes. A subscription wins when use is heavy and steady. Pay per use wins when use is light or uneven.
[800ms]
[reassuring] The outcome is that you can explain the basic ways organizations purchase and consume AI services.

## Step 10 · Stage 2 · Understand AI costs

[confident] Stage two asks: what drives the cost of AI?
[600ms]
Costs depend on how many requests are made, how large each request is, which model handles it, and how fast usage grows. A model's size and capability matter too, because more advanced models usually cost more for each request.
[700ms]
[calm] In the usage simulator, change the request volume, the request size, and the model tier. The estimate updates straight away, so you can see how each choice moves the monthly figure. Try a long request on an advanced model, and compare it with a short request on a small one.
[800ms]
[reassuring] The outcome is that you can name the common cost drivers and explain how usage influences spending.

## Step 11 · Stage 3 · Manage and optimize spending

[confident] Stage three is about control: how can we keep AI spending under control?
[600ms]
This is where FinOps for AI comes in. Finance, technology and business teams work from the same numbers. They watch consumption, set budgets and alerts, give every team ownership of its own usage, and look for waste to remove.
[700ms]
[calm] The dashboard starts with no controls, so nobody is warned as spending grows. Turn the controls on one at a time. Tagging spend by team gives visibility. A budget with an alert gives an early warning. Reusing repeated answers, using a smaller model for simple tasks, and trimming long prompts cut waste. Watch the forecast change as each one is enabled.
[800ms]
[reassuring] The outcome is that you can describe practical ways to monitor, control and optimize AI spending.

## Step 12 · Stage 4 · Measure AI business value

[confident] Stage four asks the question that matters most: is our AI investment delivering value?
[600ms]
Spending less is not the same as getting value. Value means progress toward a business objective, such as faster service, better quality or lower operating cost.
[700ms]
[calm] The interactive compares the same support process with and without AI assistance. In the second view, time per inquiry falls from twelve minutes to seven, and cost per inquiry drops from six dollars to about four. Satisfaction and first-contact resolution improve. But notice that rework went up. That is why you should look at several measures together before deciding whether it worked.
[800ms]
[reassuring] The outcome is that you can explain how to evaluate whether an AI investment supports meaningful business results.

## Step 13 · Concept · AI Economics

[confident] AI economics is the study of the costs, financial decisions and business value that come with AI.
[600ms]
[calm] In the picture, spend flows into decisions, and decisions flow into value. The idea connects three things: what you spend, the decisions you make with that spending, and the value you get back.

## Step 14 · Concept · FinOps for AI

[confident] FinOps is a collaborative way to manage and optimize cloud spending, and here it is applied to AI.
[600ms]
[calm] The diagram shows a repeating loop. You inform, by making the spend visible. You optimize, by improving it. And you operate, by keeping the improvements in place. Finance, technology and business teams all work from the same numbers, and the loop never really ends.

## Step 15 · Concept · Tokens

[confident] A token is a small piece of text that an AI model processes.
[600ms]
[calm] Often it is part of a word. In the example, the sentence "Summarize this email thread" is split into five tokens. Longer requests and longer answers use more tokens, and many services bill by tokens. The exact splitting differs between models, so treat this as an illustration.

## Step 16 · Concept · Consumption-based pricing

[confident] With consumption-based pricing, charges depend on how much of a service is used.
[600ms]
[calm] A quiet month means a small bill, and a busy month means a bigger one. The bars grow with use, and so does the bill. That flexibility is useful, but because costs follow activity, it needs monitoring as usage grows.

## Step 17 · Concept · Subscription-based pricing

[confident] Subscription-based pricing is a recurring fee for defined access or services.
[600ms]
[calm] Each seat costs the same every month, whether it is used a lot or barely at all. In the picture, only half of the seats are actively in use. The bill is easy to predict, which is the benefit, but it can be wasteful when many seats sit idle.

## Step 18 · Concept · Cost forecasting

[confident] Cost forecasting estimates future spending from expected usage and assumptions.
[600ms]
[calm] The solid columns are actual spending. The dashed columns are the forecast. A forecast is only as good as its assumptions, such as monthly growth, so write them down and revisit them as real data arrives.

## Step 19 · Concept · Observability

[confident] Observability means understanding how your AI systems operate, perform and consume resources.
[600ms]
[calm] Think of it as a set of live meters, such as requests, tokens and errors. If you cannot see them, you cannot explain a spike when it happens. Good visibility turns a surprise on the invoice into a question you can answer.

## Step 20 · Concept · Return on investment

[confident] Return on investment compares the value you gained with what it cost.
[600ms]
[calm] In the example, five thousand dollars of value minus two thousand dollars of cost, divided by two thousand, gives a return of one hundred fifty percent. The formula is simple. The hard part is the value side, which needs measures you trust rather than guesses.

## Step 21 · Concept · AI Governance

[confident] AI governance is the set of policies, responsibilities and controls that guide responsible AI use.
[600ms]
[calm] A few clear questions go a long way. Who can use it? Which data is allowed? Who owns the budget? Clear owners and simple rules help teams move quickly without losing control of cost, data or risk.

## Step 22 · Concept · Cost optimization

[confident] Cost optimization means improving efficiency and cutting unnecessary spending while keeping the outcome you want.
[600ms]
[calm] In the picture, the bar for the bill shrinks, but the outcome stays the same. The goal is not the lowest possible bill. It is the same result for less waste.
