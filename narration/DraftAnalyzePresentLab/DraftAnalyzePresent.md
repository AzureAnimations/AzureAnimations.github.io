# Draft, analyze and present with Microsoft 365 Copilot — narration (en)

## Step 1 · Five jobs, five blank pages

[confident] Five ordinary pieces of work, and every one of them starts from nothing.
[500ms]
[calm] An interview tomorrow, and a blank chat. Eight slides due for a CFO who asks hard questions. A project report that is still an empty document. And a year of sales data with no forecast attached to it.
[400ms]
[curious] None of this is hard because you are short of words.
[300ms]
[helpful] It is hard because you are starting from an empty page, in five different apps, with three useful documents sitting in OneDrive that nobody has opened.
[500ms]
[confident] This lab is about never starting from that blank page again.

## Step 2 · Setup once, then five labs

[confident] The course is one setup step and five labs, and the labs do not depend on each other.
[500ms]
[calm] Put three sample files in OneDrive once. After that you can take Lab One through Lab Five in any order you like.
[400ms]
[helpful] Each one lives in a different app. Copilot Chat to prepare an interview. PowerPoint to build an executive deck. Word to draft and restructure a report. Teams to run a launch. Excel to chart and forecast a year of sales.
[500ms]
[curious] You will notice there are no timings on these cards, and no core-or-optional split. That is deliberate. The course does not publish them, so this guide shows you the exercise count instead — twenty-five in total.
[400ms]
[confident] Everything runs in the browser. There is nothing to install and no code at any point.

## Step 3 · What you walk away with

[calm] This is the part a list of instructions never quite shows you: the things that actually exist when you are finished.
[500ms]
[confident] An interview prep page, with questions, answers and a thank-you note. An eight-slide deck built from two documents, with speaker notes written for the room. A project report, drafted then restructured. A launch meeting that already has an agenda and a facilitator. And a forecast sheet with next quarter predicted.
[500ms]
[curious] Look at what they have in common. Every one of them is grounded in a file you already had.
[400ms]
[helpful] That is the whole idea. Copilot is at its most useful when you point it at something real, rather than asking it to invent from nothing. It is also exactly why the setup step comes first — a file that lives only on your PC is invisible to it.

## Step 4 · Explore each lab

[confident] Now pick a lab, and see what it actually asks you to do.
[500ms]
[calm] Every panel is laid out the same way. A browser window showing the app you open and the address you go to. Then the prompts themselves, in order, quoted word for word from the exercise.
[400ms]
[helpful] Each prompt card tells you three things: where you are in the interface, exactly what to type, and what you should get back.
[500ms]
[curious] Watch for the highlighted words in square brackets. Those are placeholders — an interviewer's name, a company, a job title. Swap them for your own details before you send the prompt.
[400ms]
[calm] Underneath each panel is the capability that lab leaves you with, and the one warning worth reading twice — the setting people forget, or the thing that quietly will not work in a brand-new tenant.
[500ms]
[confident] Start with Setup. After that, go wherever your work actually is.

## Step 5 · What you can do afterwards

[confident] Five labs, five finished artefacts, and one habit worth keeping.
[500ms]
[calm] You can research and rehearse an interview in a single conversation. Turn two documents into an executive deck. Draft a report from a source file and restructure it in place. Set up a meeting that already has context. And explore, chart and forecast a dataset without writing a formula.
[400ms]
[curious] Along the way you will have met four agents — Idea Coach, Writing Coach, Channel Agent and Analyst — each one better at its narrow job than a single general prompt.
[500ms]
[helpful] The pattern repeats every time. Point Copilot at something real. Ask for a specific shape — eight slides, five columns, three key messages. Then edit what comes back, because the draft is a starting point and your judgement is the part that matters.
[500ms]
[confident] Every prompt on this page is quoted from the lab. Open it, and run them against your own tenant.
