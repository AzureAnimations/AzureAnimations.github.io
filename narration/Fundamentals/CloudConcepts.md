## Step 1 · Busy days and quiet days

[calm] Meet Contoso Retail. Its online shop runs on servers, computers that respond to customers and process their requests. Contoso bought enough for its busiest promotion.
[600ms]
[confident] The shop can handle that rush, but the same equipment stays there when only a few customers arrive.

## Step 2 · What happens on quiet days?

[calm] Compare the demand with the capacity Contoso owns. In this illustration, quiet days need twenty units, while a promotion needs ninety; the shop owns one hundred throughout.
[600ms]
[confident] These are capacity units, not prices. Unused capacity doesn't undo the cost of buying those servers.

## Step 3 · Pay for what you use

[calm] What if Contoso could obtain computing power, storage, and networking when it needed them? Cloud computing delivers those resources as services instead of requiring the shop to buy all the equipment first.
[600ms]
[confident] Usage is measured, and each service's pricing rules determine what Contoso pays.

## Step 4 · Match the commitment to the workload

[calm] Some shop activity changes every day; some continues steadily. Contoso compares flexible usage pricing for changing demand with commitments, such as reservations or savings plans, for eligible steady usage.
[600ms]
[confident] A lower rate can help, but the commitment remains even when Contoso uses less than expected.

## Step 5 · Public, private, or hybrid

[calm] Public cloud uses a provider's shared infrastructure; it doesn't mean customer data is public. A private cloud serves one organization. Contoso can connect private and public environments in a hybrid design.
[600ms]
[confident] The choice is about where services run and who operates them, not whether security matters.

## Step 6 · Grow and shrink with demand

[calm] Look at the two ways to add capacity. Scaling up gives one machine more power, such as more processing or memory. Scaling out adds more machines to share the work.
[600ms]
[confident] Contoso can reduce capacity when demand falls, but automatic scaling needs supported services and configured rules.

## Step 7 · Why organizations move

[calm] Contoso wants more than extra servers. It wants a shop that stays available, recovers from failures, and can be managed and protected consistently.
[600ms]
[confident] Cloud services provide useful building blocks for those goals. The benefits depend on how Contoso designs and operates the whole shop, not simply on moving it.

## Step 8 · Cloud Concepts - All in One

[calm] Contoso began with equipment sized for its busiest day. Cloud services offer another approach: choose resources, pricing, and scaling to fit the workload instead of owning peak capacity all year.
[600ms]
[confident] One question remains before choosing a service: which maintenance jobs should Contoso keep, and which should Microsoft handle?
