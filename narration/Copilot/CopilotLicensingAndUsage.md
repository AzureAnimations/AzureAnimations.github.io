## Step 1 · Four questions before Copilot goes live

[calm] Contoso is about to roll out Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Priya, the admin, has a few jobs to finish first.
[600ms]
[curious] She has to license it, control it, measure it, and keep everyone's prompts organized.
[600ms]
[confident] That's the whole lesson, and it lines up with the basic administration skills in exam A B nine hundred.

## Step 2 · Copilot and agents: what is built in

[calm] Copilot is the assistant that works across your apps, and agents are specialists that extend it.
[600ms]
[curious] Each agent brings its own instructions, knowledge and actions.
[600ms]
[confident] Researcher and Analyst are built by Microsoft and come pre-installed for licensed users, while custom agents are built by your own people.

## Step 3 · Two ways to pay for Copilot

[curious] There are two ways to pay. A license is bought per person, per month.
[600ms]
[confident] Pay-as-you-go is metered, so you pay for what's used, billed to an Azure subscription.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Copilot Chat on the web is included with Microsoft 365. Pay-as-you-go is off until an admin sets it up, and many organizations use it to learn usage patterns first.

## Step 4 · SharePoint uses both models

[calm] SharePoint is a good example of both models working together.
[600ms]
[confident] Everyday Copilot in SharePoint comes with the Copilot license, while advanced work at scale uses Copilot Credits through usage-based billing.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Admins decide who needs credits, usually content managers and site managers first, and most people stay on the license alone.

## Step 5 · Features you can switch on or off

[calm] Most Copilot settings live in the Microsoft 365 admin center.
[600ms]
[curious] A few sit in Cloud Policy or in another admin center, and Copilot Settings links you to them.
[600ms]
[serious] Be careful here. Turning off connected experiences in Cloud Policy also switches off other Microsoft 365 apps features, so use the least-privileged role, such as AI Administrator.

## Step 6 · Pick the agent that fits the job

[curious] Which agent fits the job? Researcher is for deep, cited research, and Analyst is for data and trends.
[600ms]
[confident] When the work depends on Contoso's own knowledge or process, you build a custom agent.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Researcher and Analyst are pinned for licensed users, and an admin can block them in the admin center if needed.

## Step 7 · Assign Copilot to a person or a group

[calm] First buy the licenses, then assign them on the Licenses page.
[600ms]
[confident] You can assign to one person, or to a whole group so group membership does the work.
[600ms]
[serious] Copilot is an add-on, so each person needs an eligible base plan first. You can also choose to turn on only specific apps and services.

## Step 8 · A billing policy ties usage to an Azure bill

[calm] For pay-as-you-go, you create a billing policy that links an Azure subscription to the people it covers.
[600ms]
[serious] The setup isn't complete until you connect the policy to a Copilot service. You'll need the Billing, AI or Global Administrator role.
[600ms]
[concerned] The subscription and resource group can't be edited later, so choose them carefully. Newer screens call this a spending policy.

## Step 9 · Watch the meter, and switch it off

[curious] Now watch the meter. Compare spending with the budget, in the admin center or in Azure Cost Management.
[600ms]
[confident] Budget alerts go to the groups you choose, and they can take up to twenty-four hours to arrive.
[600ms]
[serious] To stop billing, disconnect the service and then delete the policy. People lose the metered agents, so check billing afterwards.

## Step 10 · Is anyone actually using it?

[curious] Is anyone actually using it? Usage reports show who is licensed and who is active.
[600ms]
[confident] Copilot Analytics adds the Copilot Dashboard and custom reports. Enabled users hold a license, and active users tried a feature in the period you pick.
[600ms]
[reassuring] The numbers here are made up, and real report data typically appears within forty-eight hours.

## Step 11 · Prompts worth keeping

[calm] Good prompts are worth keeping. Users save, share and schedule them.
[600ms]
[confident] Admins can publish organization prompts, export what users saved, or inventory and delete scheduled prompts.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Scheduled prompts need a Copilot license, and organization prompts need the AI Administrator or Search Editor role.

## Step 12 · Which license model fits?

[curious] Pick a person and see which way to pay for them.
[600ms]
[confident] Each choice shows which Copilot features come along with it.
[600ms]
[reflective] Treat these as rules of thumb, not buying advice. Eligibility and terms change, and many organizations mix all three models.

## Step 13 · License, control, measure, organize

[calm] Let's pull it together. Know how Copilot is paid for, where it's controlled, and how to see it working.
[600ms]
[confident] And know where your prompts live, so they stay organized.
