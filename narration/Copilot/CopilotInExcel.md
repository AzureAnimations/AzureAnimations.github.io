# Narrator Script — Journey · Microsoft 365 Copilot in Excel

**Source animation:** `static/v2/Copilot/CopilotInExcel.html`

Tags in `[brackets]` are delivery cues — speaking styles (e.g. `[confident]`),
paralinguistics (e.g. `[laughter]`), and `[NNNms]` pause markers (e.g. `[600ms]`).
One block per animation step.

---

## Step 1 · Thousands of rows, not one conclusion

[calm] The proposal is approved and the deck is built — now the steering committee wants the numbers.
[600ms]
[concerned] Alex opens Support Metrics F Y twenty-six dot xlsx: twelve thousand four hundred and eighty rows, twelve months, three regions, four products.
[600ms]
[curious] Every fact is in there, and not one line says what it means.
[800ms]
[confident] So Copilot opens beside the sheet — not instead of it.

## Step 2 · Ask it the way you’d ask a colleague

[curious] No PivotTable, no VLOOKUP, no nested IF — so where does Alex even start?
[800ms]
[confident] By typing the question in ordinary English. Which region performs best?
[600ms]
[impressed] EMEA — four point two hours to first response and ninety-one percent CSAT. Then a follow-up: which product has the most tickets? Contoso Devices, with four thousand eight hundred and twelve.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Copilot reads the rows already in the sheet, and keeps the context for whatever Alex asks next.

## Step 3 · Turn twelve months into one picture

[determined] One prompt: analyse support ticket trends.
[600ms]
[confident] Copilot answers with something Alex can actually look at — a chart or a PivotTable, built from the Tickets sheet.
[600ms]
[impressed] Volume climbs gently from July, jumps in September, and never settles back — forty-one percent above the twelve-month average.
[800ms]
[calm] The chart isn’t the answer. The sentence beside the chart is.

## Step 4 · The spike nobody had noticed

[curious] Ask for anything unusual, and Copilot points straight at it.
[800ms]
[impressed] September: one thousand two hundred and four tickets, against a monthly average of eight hundred and fifty-four.
[600ms]
[intrigued] The surge starts the week Contoso Devices 3 shipped, and four in five of the extra tickets are setup questions.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Copilot highlights those rows in the table itself, so Alex can sort and filter straight to them.

## Step 5 · Where the line is heading

[confident] Alex asks Copilot where next quarter’s support volume is heading.
[600ms]
[calm] The line runs solid where there’s data and dashed beyond it — around two thousand nine hundred tickets if nothing changes.
[600ms]
[serious] Copilot reasons from the numbers in the workbook. It isn’t a statistical forecasting engine, and it says so.
[800ms]
[reflective] Read the dashed line as a direction, and deciding whether to believe it is still Alex’s job.

## Step 6 · The four lines the executives actually read

[determined] The last prompt is the one that matters: summarise these findings for leadership.
[600ms]
[confident] Copilot compresses the chart, the outlier and the trend into four lines for the steering committee.
[600ms]
[impressed] Volume up forty-one percent since September; the surge began with the Contoso Devices 3 launch; EMEA still answers fastest at four point two hours, APAC slowest at nine point six.
[800ms]
[proud] And the fourth line is a recommendation — fund the AI support assistant now, starting with setup questions.

## Step 7 · From a spreadsheet nobody read to a recommendation leadership can act on

[proud] A handful of questions, no formulas, one afternoon.
[600ms]
[confident] Ask, look, question, summarise — all inside Excel, in plain language.
[600ms]
[optimistic] Without Copilot that’s pivot tables, complex formulas and manual analysis. With it, the numbers behind the AI support platform finally say something out loud.
[800ms]
[encouraging] Excel is where the evidence lives — and Alex is still the analyst, and still the one who decides.
