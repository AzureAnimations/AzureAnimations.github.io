## Step 1 · Can we trust this cloud with our data?

[calm] Northwind Traders is about to trust Contoso with its data, and Contoso runs on Microsoft's cloud.
[600ms]
[curious] Jo is the auditor for Northwind, and she asks Priya, the compliance lead at Contoso, for evidence. A promise isn't enough.
[600ms]
[confident] This lesson covers the Service Trust Portal and Microsoft's privacy principles, two skills from S C nine hundred.

## Step 2 · Where Microsoft publishes its proof

[calm] The Microsoft Trust Center explains Microsoft's privacy commitments in plain language.
[600ms]
[confident] The Service Trust Portal is the public site where Microsoft publishes audit reports and other compliance information.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Some resources ask you to sign in with a Microsoft Entra organization account and accept a non-disclosure agreement for compliance materials.

## Step 3 · Inside the Service Trust Portal

[curious] The portal home page groups its content into four areas, so you can find the right kind of document quickly.
[600ms]
[calm] Save the documents you need to My Library, and you can get an email when one is updated.
[600ms]
[serious] Restricted documents need a role, such as Compliance Administrator or Security Reader, and the tile names can change over time.

## Step 4 · Pick a report, read it like an auditor

[curious] Choose a standard tile, narrow the list by cloud service or date, and open a document.
[600ms]
[confident] An audit report tells an auditor who checked what, and which services were covered.
[600ms]
[calm] Many files ask you to accept a license agreement first. The Regional Resources tile holds documents about regional regulations.

## Step 5 · Compliance offerings: regulation to service

[calm] Start from the rule you must follow. Find the matching offering, and open its folder in the portal.
[600ms]
[curious] Then check which Microsoft cloud services the audit actually covered.
[600ms]
[serious] You're responsible for your own compliance. Microsoft documentation isn't legal advice, so ask your legal advisor about regulations.

## Step 6 · Microsoft’s four privacy principles

[confident] Microsoft's approach to data privacy rests on four areas: data control, data location, data security and data defense.
[600ms]
[calm] Each one is a commitment, not just a feature.
[600ms]
[reassuring] Those promises are backed by Microsoft's standard contracts for commercial and public sector customers.

## Step 7 · Match each question to a principle

[curious] Customers rarely say privacy principle. They ask plain questions, like who is able to see my data.
[600ms]
[calm] Follow each question to the principle that answers it.
[600ms]
[intrigued] Notice that two questions can share one principle. Control covers deleting data and advertising. Defense covers who receives a request, and whether you're told.

## Step 8 · From evidence to action

[calm] The portal shows what Microsoft does. But your auditor also wants to know what you do.
[600ms]
[confident] Compliance Manager turns a standard into an assessment. Microsoft's part comes with audit results, and your part becomes actions with owners.
[600ms]
[reassuring] It's a Microsoft Purview solution, and available assessments depend on your licensing agreement.

## Step 9 · Which tool answers my auditor?

[excited] Click a question an auditor might ask. The matching tool lights up, with what you would do there.
[600ms]
[curious] Some questions point to the Service Trust Portal. Others point to Compliance Manager.
[600ms]
[calm] A few lead to other Purview tools, like labels, data loss prevention and eDiscovery.

## Step 10 · Prove it, promise it, act on it

[calm] You can now say where Microsoft publishes proof, and what its privacy principles promise.
[600ms]
[confident] You can also explain how that evidence turns into your own compliance work.
[600ms]
[proud] That's the S C nine hundred skill set for this area. The skills measured change over time, so always read the current study guide.
