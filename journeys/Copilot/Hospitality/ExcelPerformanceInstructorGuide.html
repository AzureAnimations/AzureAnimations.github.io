The Riverside Grand story

A fictional hotel with 220 rooms, used in every Hospitality session. Tell this in about a minute, in your own words. The session starts with it.

Who is Riverside Grand?

A fictional hotel with 220 rooms. Every Hospitality session uses it. You are its Hotel Operations Coordinator.

220 rooms Every night from 1 January to 31 March 2027

5 workbooks Performance · guests · facilities · service · reservations

Synthetic data Every hotel, guest and booking is made up for training

Why look at the numbers?

The hotel wants to know where it is under pressure before it acts.

Eight pressures show up in the workbooks: uneven demand, facility queues, downtime, maintenance-stock gaps, food-service variation, housekeeping pressure, parking and lobby congestion, reservation-system friction.

Script (60–90 seconds)

Let me introduce today’s hotel. Riverside Grand is a made-up hotel with 220 rooms, and you are its Hotel Operations Coordinator.

There are five workbooks covering the first three months of 2027: daily performance, guests, facilities, service and reservations. Every value in them is synthetic.

Eight pressures show up across the files, from uneven demand to reservation friction. Your job is not to fix them today. It is to ask better questions and trace every answer to a row.

Copilot can help analyse. The hotel team verifies. In the next hour you will use the app for your session, and learn to spot when an answer goes beyond the evidence.

Ask the room. When a hotel looks busy and guests are unhappy, what would you check first?

Watch for. Point at the eight pressures and the 220 rooms. Remind the room that every number is fictional and every answer needs checking.

How to use Presenter view

Open it
Press N, or click the Presenter button (bottom left of the lesson, next to the outline button). A small window opens. Drag it to your own screen so learners never see it.
If it does not open
Your browser may block pop-ups. Then the notes open in a panel over the lesson instead, and learners can see it if you share this window. Allow pop-ups for this site to get the separate window. Press N again or Esc to close the panel.
Use it
Each step shows what to say, one question for the room, what to watch for, and what comes next. Prev and Next in the window move the lesson. The clock counts up to 75:00.
Take it away
The button “Download all presenter notes (.txt)” saves every step’s notes. The printable Instructor Guide has the same notes.

Before the session

  • Everyone has Microsoft 365 Copilot and can open Excel and Copilot Chat. Check this a day before.
  • The five sample workbooks are saved in OneDrive or SharePoint, with AutoSave on. Check in your own environment that Copilot can open them.
  • You have tested one prompt in Copilot Chat with files 01 and 03 attached, so you know what a good answer looks like.
  • Nobody opens the earlier all-in-one hotel workbook. Learners analyse one focused file at a time in Excel. Copilot Chat may combine two selected files. Never all five at once.
  • Open the lesson in one window and the Presenter view in another. Press F for full screen on the shared window.

Words this session uses

Signal. A number or pattern in the data that deserves a closer look. It is not yet a finding.
Counterexample. A case that does not fit your story, such as a busy day with excellent service.
Evidence. Proof you can point to: a workbook, a sheet and a row.
Sample size. How many records sit behind a number. A small group can swing an average.
Association. Two things moving together. It does not show that one caused the other.
RevPAR. Revenue per available room: the average room rate (ADR) times the share of rooms sold.

Step by step: what to say, ask and watch

1.Hotel Performance: the plan for today1 min

Say

Welcome to a hands-on session with Copilot on hotel performance.

The strip shows the seven stages we will work through: orient, question, scan, connect, chat, challenge and verify.

Copilot can help analyse. The hotel team verifies.

Ask the roomWho has been handed a crowded workbook and asked what it means? What did you do first?

Watch forPoint at the seven stages from left to right. Say now that two of them, Challenge and Verify, are mandatory.

2.Meet Riverside Grand in plain words2 min

Say

Let me introduce today’s hotel. Riverside Grand is a made-up hotel with 220 rooms, and you are its Hotel Operations Coordinator.

There are five workbooks covering the first three months of 2027: daily performance, guests, facilities, service and reservations. Every value in them is synthetic.

Ask the roomWhen a hotel looks busy and guests are unhappy, what would you check first?

Watch forPoint at the eight pressures and the 220 rooms. Remind the room that every number is fictional and every answer needs checking.

3.Riverside Grand at a glance1 min

Say

This is Riverside Grand in numbers, all from file 01.

Occupancy averaged 73.7 percent over 90 days, the average guest rating was 4.39 and there were 134 complaints.

Every label has a plain word beside it, so read that word out. These are averages and totals, not a verdict.

Ask the roomWhich of these six numbers would you check first, and which row would you open?

Watch forDo not let anyone read the cost or revenue as profit. If asked, say we will not build a profit story from this file.

4.Your role and your job today1 min

Say

You are the Hotel Operations Coordinator. The general manager wants to know where the hotel is under pressure, and what to look at first.

You will make three things: a performance scan, an evidence grid and a checked briefing.

Two modes run all lesson: Copilot in Excel on one file, and Copilot Chat on the web with one or two files and no Excel open.

Ask the roomWhy might we keep the two modes visibly apart?

Watch forPoint at the two mode badges. Every do-step shows one of them. Say it plainly: never all five files at once.

5.Five focused files, one question at a time2 min

Say

Five focused files: each has a job and a warning, so read the card before you open the file.

The conflict at the bottom is real: file 02 says groups have unequal sample sizes, but its own summary shows 36 responses in every segment.

We say what the rows show.

Ask the roomIf a sheet describes its data one way and the rows say another, which do you trust, and what do you do next?

Watch forFile 05 is only for the optional stretch. Make sure nobody opens a file that is not on the card list.

6.Download the five workbooks1 min

Say

Now we get the files.

Click a card to download one workbook, or take all five in the ZIP.

Everything is made-up data, so it is safe to download.

Ask the roomIs anyone blocked from downloading files on this computer?

Watch forA locked-down laptop may block downloads. Find out now, not at minute 30.

7.Save them in OneDrive, AutoSave on1 min

Say

Copilot in Excel needs the workbook saved in OneDrive or SharePoint, with AutoSave on.

Create a folder called Hotel Performance and upload the five files.

Your setup may look different, so check in your own environment.

Ask the roomDoes everyone have a OneDrive folder they can open right now?

Watch forThe usual stall is a learner who left the files in Downloads. Walk the room and look for the folder on each screen.

8.Explore file 01 before you ask3 min

Say

Before we ask Copilot anything, we look at the file.

File 01 has three sheets and 90 daily rows. Look at the Day column: it does not match the dates, so we quote dates, not weekdays.

Ask the roomWhat would you want the sheet to tell you that it does not?

Watch forPoint at the four limits at the bottom. The Day label and the cost warning are the two learners miss.

9.Build one question before you ask5 min

Say

A good question says what you will decide, what you will compare, and what the file cannot show.

Click one choice in each group and watch one sentence take shape.

Read the rule at the bottom aloud: describe timing and observed relationships, and do not say caused by unless the workbook supports it.

Ask the roomWhich of the six parts do you usually leave out of a question?

Watch forLearners often skip the limitation. Ask them to change it and read the new sentence aloud.

10.Useful prompt or leading prompt?3 min

Say

Now the other side. A leading prompt asks Copilot to agree with you.

Click the leading prompt in each pair, then read why.

A useful prompt asks Copilot to test the idea, with the evidence for it, against it and missing.

Ask the roomHave you ever asked a question that already contained the answer you wanted?

Watch forThe second pair is about guest value. Say clearly that segment, tier, room type and channel never define it.

11.Open Copilot in Excel and find your file1 min

Say

Open the workbook in Excel and click Copilot.

Type a forward slash to see how you point at a file.

The workbook must be in OneDrive or SharePoint. Check the exact method in your own environment.

Ask the roomWhere did you save your files, and can Copilot see them?

Watch forThe common mistake is working on a copy in Downloads. Look for the OneDrive path in the title bar.

12.Try Out 1 · Map the file, then scan it4 min

Say

This is Try Out 1, in Copilot in Excel on file 01.

Paste Try this first, to map the file. Then paste Then ask, to scan performance.

Read the Verify line before you send anything.

Ask the roomWhat did Copilot name first, and does that row exist?

Watch forWalk the room and look for anyone accepting the first answer without opening a row.

13.Try Out 1 · Check the anomaly log4 min

Say

Now we check Copilot against the rows.

Click Verify with workbook on each day and compare the source row with what Copilot wrote.

A status chip is earned by the row, not by how sure Copilot sounds.

Ask the roomWhich of the five days would you drop, and why?

Watch forPoint at the Day column in the source row. It does not match the date, so we quote dates only.

14.Try Out 1 · Reveal and discuss2 min

Say

Now reveal the two discussion cards, one at a time.

Day 23 is 23 January, and Day 58 is 27 February. They have similar demand and opposite service.

A quiet day can still have a queue, so hotel-wide occupancy is an incomplete measure.

Ask the roomWhat would you check next on each of these days?

Watch forDo not reveal both at once. Let the room guess the card before you click.

15.Checkpoint 1 · Scan done, ready to connect1 min

Say

This is a checkpoint, a pause to see where we are.

We have oriented, questioned and scanned.

Next we connect performance to service.

Ask the roomWhat is one thing you would double-check before moving on?

Watch forPoint at the NEXT badge on the strip and the progress bar.

16.Try Out 2 · Connect service operations4 min

Say

Try Out 2 is in Copilot in Excel again, now on file 03.

Stay on this one file. It has three related sheets: usage, downtime and maintenance.

Paste the Try prompt, then the suggested follow-up, which is not from the course pack.

Ask the roomWhich sheet do you expect to matter most?

Watch forWatch for anyone attaching a second file. In Excel we use one file at a time.

17.Try Out 2 · Check the evidence table3 min

Say

Copilot has built an evidence table for three claims.

Look at each claim for evidence for it, evidence against it and what is missing.

A claim with no contradicting evidence is not finished.

Ask the roomWhich claim has the weakest evidence?

Watch forGym has the most downtime but a short wait. That is the counterexample to find.

18.Try Out 2 · Fill the evidence grid5 min

Say

Now the evidence grid: click each claim to read all seven fields and give it a status.

Then reveal the pool trail and the arrival congestion.

Both warrant investigation, and neither proves a cause.

Ask the roomWhat is the one record you would ask the team for first?

Watch forLearners want to say the pump fault caused the pool queue. Point at the contradicting evidence on the reveal.

19.Checkpoint 2 · Connected, ready for Chat1 min

Say

Another checkpoint.

We have an evidence grid with a status for every claim.

Next we move to Copilot Chat and brief the manager, without opening Excel.

Ask the roomWhich status did you use most, and why?

Watch forPoint at the NEXT badge. Chat is the next stage.

20.Try Out 3 · Brief the manager in Copilot Chat4 min

Say

Try Out 3 is a different mode: Copilot Chat on the web, with Excel closed.

Type a slash, attach files 01 and 03, then paste the Try prompt.

Two files at most. The approved pairs are 01 and 03, 02 and 03, or 02 and 04.

Ask the roomWhat does Copilot need from you to cite a sheet?

Watch forCheck the mode badge on screen. Anyone with Excel open is in the wrong mode.

21.Try Out 3 · Check the briefing, sheet by sheet5 min

Say

Here is the one-page briefing, point by point.

Tick a point only when its sheet and row are named. For the rest, use Verify later in the workbook.

If Chat cannot open the file, use the prepared output and keep discussing the evidence.

Ask the roomWhich point has no sheet, and what should happen to it?

Watch forThe lift sensor point carries an assumption label. Ask the room what the file does not say.

22.Checkpoint 3 · Briefed, ready to challenge1 min

Say

Third checkpoint.

We have a source-linked briefing.

Next is Refine, and it is mandatory.

Ask the roomWhat would make you trust this briefing less?

Watch forPoint at the NEXT badge. Challenge and Verify are the last two stages.

23.Refine · Challenge bias and overconfidence3 min

Say

Refine is mandatory. We challenge a draft that sounds sure of itself.

Copy the draft, paste it into a new Copilot Chat with files 02 and 03, then paste the Refine prompt.

The follow-up is a suggestion, not from the course pack.

Ask the roomWhich sentence in the draft worries you most?

Watch forThe draft is overconfident on purpose. Do not let anyone treat it as a real colleague.

24.Refine · Find the unsupported language4 min

Say

Click Highlight unsupported language and read the labels.

Each highlighted word claims a cause, a certainty or a value that the rows do not show.

Then read the supported rewrite, and check its figures yourself.

Ask the roomWhich highlighted word would you remove first?

Watch forEvery label has words as well as colour. Read the labels aloud.

25.Refine · Six bias and causation cards3 min

Say

Six cards: sample size, proxy bias, confirmation bias, missingness, causation and operational context.

Click each one to test the draft against the files.

Then reveal the counterexample and the bias correction.

Ask the roomWhich card caught the most in the draft?

Watch forGroups here are equal in size. The risk is one record, a zero that is really missing, and groups of five to eight records.

26.Optional stretch · Reservation journey3 min

Say

This stretch is optional. It is counted in the 75 minutes.

Open file 05 in Excel, and paste Prompt 7.

If you are behind, skip it and give the time back to Refine.

Ask the roomDo we have time for the stretch, or do we stay with Refine?

Watch forDecide before you click. Skipping cleanly is part of the plan.

27.Optional stretch · Check the reservation friction4 min

Say

Compare rates by channel, not counts.

The extreme payment failure is real, but it sits in the channel with the lowest error rate.

One status conflict stays visible, because the fields agree and only the note disagrees.

Ask the roomHow many records stand behind each stage conclusion?

Watch forEach abandon stage rests on five to eight records. Say so out loud.

28.Final verification · Prompt 81 min

Say

Final verification is mandatory, and it is short.

Paste your revised analysis into Copilot Chat with files 02 and 03, then Prompt 8.

Copilot checks every number, date, record and recommendation.

Ask the roomWhat do you expect Copilot to find?

Watch forWatch the clock. This is one minute, then straight to the verdict.

29.Final verification · Ready or Not Ready1 min

Say

Read the verdict. It is Not Ready.

Two figures and one record ID have no source location, and the contradicting evidence is missing.

Final status cannot be Ready while any material figure lacks a source location.

Ask the roomWhich fix would you make first?

Watch forLearners often want Ready. Tick the fixes, then say that only a second Prompt 8 run can change the status.

30.Review · The six questions

Say

Run your result through the same six questions every time.

Mark each one for yourself. Nothing is stored or sent.

Completing the checks does not approve the result.

Ask the roomWhich question did your result struggle with most?

Watch forThis step rides inside the last three minutes. Read it, do not discuss it.

31.Your prompt pack

Say

Copy any prompt from the pack, or download everything.

The pack names the file each prompt uses, and how to verify it.

Prompts 4 and 7 are optional. Try one on your own data this week.

Ask the roomWhich prompt will you reuse first?

Watch forPoint at the Use and Verify lines before anyone copies.

32.What you learned today1 min

Say

Here is what you can do now: scan, connect, brief, challenge and verify.

This workshop is not about asking AI for a dashboard. It is about asking better operational questions, tracing every answer to evidence, challenging bias and causation, and deciding what to investigate next.

Copilot proposes, and the learner verifies.

Ask the roomWhich report at work could you trace back to its rows this week?

Watch forLet the closing question sit for a moment, then point at the hub button.
