The Meridian story (the same in every session)

Fictional regional retailer of consumer electronics and home accessories. Tell this in about a minute, in your own words. Every session starts with it.

Who is Meridian?

A fictional regional retailer of electronics and home accessories, with an online store and 3 distribution centres.

18 shops Singapore 8 · Malaysia 6 · Thailand 4

SGD 151.8m sales in FY2026, up 20.1% (not yet audited)

SGD 5.5m profit before tax, down 28.6%

Why is an audit team here?

Meridian’s board meets on 20 February 2027 and wants its FY2026 numbers (the year to 31 December 2026) checked first.

You have just joined the engagement team. It will: look at the records, ask questions, collect evidence, report what needs fixing.

Script (60–90 seconds)

Let me introduce today’s client. Meridian is a made-up regional retailer. It sells consumer electronics and home accessories, in 18 shops across Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand, plus an online store and three distribution centres.

Last year was strange. Sales went up 20.1 percent, to SGD 151.8 million. But profit before tax fell 28.6 percent, to SGD 5.5 million. More sales, less profit. And the stock in the warehouses and the money customers owe grew even faster than sales.

A lot changed along the way. A new online-store system went live in July, the way stock is valued changed in September, and the supervisor who handled supplier payments left in October.

Meridian’s board meets on 20 February 2027. It wants its FY2026 numbers checked first. So an audit team looks at the records, asks questions, collects evidence, and reports what needs fixing.

You have just joined that team, and Copilot is your assistant. Copilot proposes. You check. In the next hour you will use Copilot in the app for your session, and learn how to spot when it gets something wrong.

Ask the room. Who has been on either side of an audit, or worked in retail, and when sales go up but profit falls, where would you look first?

Watch for. Point at the two numbers on screen: sales up, profit down. Read the “words you will meet” out loud once, so nobody feels lost later. If someone asks whether Meridian is real, say no: it is fictional training data.

Words this session uses

Engagement team. The group of auditors working on one client. You have just joined it.
Finding. A problem the team spotted, written up: what happened, why it matters, what to do.
Evidence. Proof you can point to: a document, a record or a number from the files.
Inconsistency. Two places that say different things about the same fact.
Executive summary. A short note for busy leaders. Only the main points.
Source file. The document an answer came from, so you can check it.

13.Checkpoint 1 · Ready for Try Out 21 min

Next is Try 2: from messy interview notes to a finding.

Ask the roomFrom one to five, how sure are you that your figures match the file?

14.Try Out 2 · From interview notes to a finding8 min

Say

Try 2: turn interview notes into evidence.

Open 02_Audit_Interview_Notes.docx and ask Copilot for a table with four columns: statement, who said it, possible risk, and evidence to request.

Then ask for one finding with three headings: condition, risk and recommendation.

Ask the roomWhy do you think the table needs a 'who said it' column?

Watch forInterview notes are raw, so the table has to stay inside what was said. Watch for anyone who accepts a number Copilot added. Fast finishers can try the optional stretch.

15.Try Out 2 · Check the table and the finding4 min

Say

Check the table first.

Match every row to the person who said it. What they said is not proof until evidence backs it up.

Then check that the finding uses only facts from 02_Audit_Interview_Notes.docx.

Ask the roomWhich row would you ask for evidence on first?

Watch forEvery row should still show the Unresolved chip. Point at the warning: 'about 430' is what someone said, not a counted number. Look for any invented number, name or date.

16.Optional stretch · Rewrite finding F-02Optional · 3 min

Say

This one is optional, for people who finish early.

Open 03_Audit_Findings_Draft.docx, pick finding F-02, and ask Copilot to rewrite it in neutral, professional language with a risk rating.

A rating is a judgement. Copilot can propose one, and you decide.

Ask the roomWould you accept the rating Copilot proposes? What would you need to see first?

Watch forThere's no timer and no pressure, so don't let it slow the main group. The proposed rating is labelled as a proposal on screen, and the blank date stays visible.

17.Checkpoint 2 · Both Try Outs are done1 min

Say

Second stop. Both Try Outs are done.

Next is Refine, and it's mandatory: we write the executive summary.

It's the part busy leaders actually read.

Ask the roomWhat's one habit from the last two Try Outs that you'll keep?

Watch forEveryone should reach Refine, so look for anyone still stuck in Try 2 and help them now. The self-check is about rows that should still be marked Unresolved.

18.Refine · Write the executive summary6 min

Say

Refine: write the executive summary.

Open 03_Audit_Findings_Draft.docx and paste the Refine prompt. It asks for the three biggest matters, how much money is at risk and what evidence is still missing, in under 150 words.

Then paste the follow-up that asks Copilot to list anything it can't support.

Ask the roomWhat would a busy leader want from 150 words?

Watch forThis step is mandatory. The word limit is the point, so look for summaries that run long. The follow-up is where a first answer becomes a checked answer.

19.Refine · Check the summary3 min

Say

Now check the summary against 03_Audit_Findings_Draft.docx, line by line.

Only one finding has an amount, SGD 1.25 million, so any bigger total is made up.

Remove anything you can't support.

Ask the roomWhich sentence in the summary could you not point to in 03_Audit_Findings_Draft.docx?

Watch forPoint at the date warning: 03_Audit_Findings_Draft.docx gives finding F-05 a target date of 28 February 2027, but the board meets on 20 February. We flag it. We don't fix it quietly.

20.Review · The six questions2 min

Say

Last check: the six questions. Source, evidence, status, audience, action and professional boundary.

Ask them of every result you plan to use.

Finishing the checks doesn't approve the result, because a person decides.

Ask the roomWhich of the six questions would catch the mistake you are most likely to make?

Watch for'Professional boundary' is the one people don't know. It means no audit opinion and no claim that management agreed. Point at that row. This step is mandatory.

21.Your prompt pack1 min

Say

This is your prompt pack. Copy any prompt, or download everything.

It has three prompts, three follow-ups and the stretch task, so you can reuse them at work.

Ask the roomWhich prompt would you try first on a real document at work?

Watch forRemind people of the ground rule: no personal or client data in prompts. Point at the download buttons so nobody leaves without the pack.

22.What you learned today2 min

Say

Today you did three things with Copilot in Word.

You briefed yourself on a client, turned interview notes into evidence, and wrote a summary you checked.

Keep three habits: check against the source, keep the gaps visible, and let a person decide.

Ask the roomWhich document at work could you check this week, using the six questions?

Watch forLeave the closing question on screen and let people answer. Point at the buttons for the next session. Say it once more: Copilot proposes, the team verifies.

Words this session uses

Journal entry. One line in the company’s accounting records, in the General_Ledger sheet. Meridian has 1,800.
Manual journal. An entry typed in by a person, not created automatically by a till, online shop or system.
Self-approved. The same person prepared and approved the entry, so no second person signed it off.
Flag. A marker that says look here. It is a pointer for checking, not proof of anything.
Related party. A business closely linked to the company or its leaders. Deals with it get extra checking.
Dormant vendor. A supplier marked as inactive. A payment to one needs a good explanation.
